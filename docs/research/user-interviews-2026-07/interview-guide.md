# AI Stack user interviews — guide & outreach kit (2026-07)

Ticket: https://github.com/alp82/aistack/issues/4 (part of the direction/USP map, #3)

**Goal.** Understand why current users don't curate their stacks, what (if anything) would bring them back, and whether a share-worthy moment exists. Findings feed the identity/USP decision (#7).

**Who.** 8–12 of the ~24 registered users. Mix deliberately:
- ~6–8 who created a stack but haven't edited it since (the silent majority)
- ~2–3 who did come back / edit / upvote (the outliers — learn what pulled them)
- 1–2 CLI users if identifiable (the `collect`/`create` loop is the shipping USP candidate)

**Format.** Async DM answers are fine; offer a 15-minute call as the upgrade. Async lowers the bar and these users are already on Discord.

---

## Interview guide (7 questions, non-leading)

Ask in order; follow up with "tell me more" / "what happened then" rather than new topics. Don't pitch features, don't explain what the site is *supposed* to be for, don't defend anything.

1. **Last visit.** "Walk me through the last time you opened aistack.to — what brought you there that day, and what did you end up doing?"
2. **Original hope.** "Think back to when you first made your stack page. What were you hoping would happen after you published it?"
3. **The real alternative.** "Outside the site — how do you currently decide on and keep track of the AI tools you use? Where does that actually happen today?"
4. **Change moments.** "When your real AI setup changes — you adopt a tool, drop one, switch models — what do you do about it, if anything, and where?"
5. **Sharing.** "Have you ever shown or sent your stack page (or the site) to anyone? What was the situation?" *(If no: leave it — don't ask why not; the silence is the data.)*
6. **Consumption side.** "Have you ever looked at someone else's stack on the site? What were you looking for, and did you find it?"
7. **Disappearance test.** "If aistack.to shut down tomorrow, what — if anything — would you miss?"

Optional closer if energy remains:

8. **Open pain.** "What's the most annoying part of managing your AI tooling right now, anywhere in your workflow?"

### What each question is probing (for the synthesizer, not the interviewee)

| Q | Probes |
|---|--------|
| 1 | Real recency & trigger — is there *any* return loop today? |
| 2 | The original job-to-be-done they hired the site for |
| 3 | The true competitor (Twitter, a Notion doc, nothing) |
| 4 | The curation trigger that exists in life but isn't captured by the product |
| 5 | Whether a share moment exists → virality / consultant-identity signal |
| 6 | Browse/compare value → stack-curation & learning-hub identity signal |
| 7 | Retention floor — what's actually valued vs. tolerated |
| 8 | Adjacent pains → open USP space |

## DM script (Discord)

> Hey <name> — Alper here, I build aistack.to. You made a stack on it a while back (thanks!). I'm deciding where to take the product next and I'd rather ask the ~20 real users than guess.
>
> Could I ask you 7 short questions? Answer right here in DM whenever suits you, or if you prefer, a 15-min call. No wrong answers — "I forgot the site existed" is genuinely useful data.

If they accept async, paste questions **one at a time** (batching gets one-word answers).

### Subreddit / Discord channel fallback post

> I'm planning the next phase of aistack.to and want real input from people who actually made a stack. If you have 10 minutes for 7 questions (DM or a short call), reply here or DM me — every answer directly shapes what gets built.

## Capturing notes

- One comment per interviewee on issue #4: alias (u1, u2, …), segment (dormant / returner / CLI), then raw-ish answers per question number. Verbatim quotes where they're sharp.
- Don't summarize while collecting — synthesis is the agent's job afterwards.

## Done when

- 8+ responses captured on the ticket (or fewer if the channel is exhausted — say so).
- Then run `/wayfinder` on the ticket: the agent synthesizes the 3–5 sharpest insights into a linked summary and resolves #4.
